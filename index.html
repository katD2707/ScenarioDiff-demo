Abstract

Textual context such as news, reports, and logs can signal changes before they appear in a numerical time series. ScenarioDiff organizes this context into three levels of guidance: stepwise historical evidence, a qualitative forecast scenario, and sparse future anchor intervals. A multimodal diffusion forecaster uses the historical summaries and scenario description to generate future trajectories. Anchor Blended Sampling then locally refines event-relevant regions without retraining the model. On the Time-MMD benchmark, the clearest gains occur in event-driven domains where external documents contain useful clues about future changes.

Why scenario-level guidance?

A model that sees only past values can follow regular trends while missing an event described in a recent document. Raw documents are also noisy and weakly aligned with the time series. ScenarioDiff makes the role of each piece of text explicit before asking a numerical model to forecast.

How ScenarioDiff works

The paper separates observed evidence, future hypotheses, and local trajectory constraints. Only information available before the forecast origin enters the three agents.

The agents produce guidance rather than a complete numerical forecast. The diffusion model generates possible trajectories, and the anchor bands act as soft constraints during a short inference-time refinement.

Results

ScenarioDiff is evaluated on five Time-MMD domains. In the paper's event-driven group (Economy, Energy, and Security), it has the best average MSE/MAE rank among the reported methods. The paper also reports 13 first-place horizon-level MSE/MAE entries.
